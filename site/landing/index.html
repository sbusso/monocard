The darkroom for monochrome sensors.

Monocard is a free, open source raw editor for cameras with no color filter on the sensor. It develops one channel, from the sensor data to the print. The first camera is the Ricoh GR IV Monochrome.

Download for macOS Version 0.3.0. macOS 13.4 or later, Apple silicon. Notarized. Also for Linux and Windows.

Develop
One channel, raw to print. A monochrome camera’s JPEG is edited with the same tools.
Color
Color photos open in grey, through a black and white film filter. No color tool is shown anywhere in the window.
Speed
A slider redraws in 3 to 6 ms at 26 MP (6192 × 4128) on an M5 Max.

IZone view

Key Z paints the picture in eleven zones, 0 to X, in equal steps of lightness. Zone V is the 18 % grey card. The scale at the edge of this page is the same one.

Drag the line across the frame. The picture is on its left, its zones on its right. Arrow keys move it when it has focus; a double click puts it back.Drag the line.

IINoise

Denoise works from a noise model of the sensor at each ISO, on the one channel the sensor recorded. A slider keeps part of the sensor’s grain.

Profiled
A noise model for each ISO, with non-local means or wavelets.
Raw wavelets
Wavelet denoise on the raw data.
Keep grain
Leaves a chosen part of the sensor’s grain in the picture.

IIITone

Dodge and burn by zone. The tone equalizer has nine zones, from −8 EV to 0 EV, and follows edges, to avoid halos. The curve and the LIGHT sliders stay on screen.

LIGHT
Nine sliders, one a stop, from −8 EV to 0 EV. A zone mask. The zone view switch.
BASIC
Exposure, contrast, blacks, midtones, whites. Display transform: sigmoid or AgX.
Curve
Drawn over the histogram. Presets: linear, soft S, strong S, lifted, faded.

IVDetail

Contrast by scale, from fine texture to large forms, and sharpening at the sensor’s pixel pitch.

Local contrast
Contrast between neighbouring areas.
Contrast equalizer
One control for each scale, from fine texture to large forms.
Diffuse or sharpen
Deblurs detail at the sensor’s pixel pitch.

VHighlights

Clipped light is lost. A monochrome sensor has no other channel to take it from. Monocard shows what the sensor clipped and shapes the way into white.

Clip map
Key C marks what the sensor clipped, apart from what the display clips.
Shoulder
A roll-off into white under the clip point.
Bracket merge
Merges 2 to 5 frames of one scene to keep more range.

VIMasks

Five kinds of mask. Each drives exposure, the tone equalizer, the curve and local contrast.

Brush
Painted by hand.
Gradient
A straight line, or an ellipse.
Luminance
A range of tones.
Subject
Found by a model.
Sky
Found by a model.

VIILooks

Ten looks, each a small text file, each editable after it is applied. Grain follows the tones. Toning and solarize are part of a look.

The frame below is one picture in eleven bands: no look, then the ten looks, in the order of the LOOK panel.

IXCamera window

Connect the camera by USB and its window opens. It shows the photos on the camera in a grid, by day. Select the ones to keep and import them.

Opens
When a camera is connected, or from the menu.
Import
The selected photos only.
Delete
Nothing is deleted on the camera. There is no delete button.
System
macOS.

XOutput

A picture that is not toned leaves as a grey file, one channel. The proof shows it within the white and the black of a paper.

Files
Grey JPEG, 16-bit grey TIFF.
Paper proof
Matte, baryta, newsprint.
Sidecar
The edit is kept beside the photo, in IMG.DNG.mcd.
Command line
monocard develops files with no window.

Limits

  • The noise profile for the Ricoh GR IV Monochrome is not calibrated.
  • The learned denoiser is not trained. The profiled and the wavelet denoisers work now.
  • Clipped light is lost. Monocard shapes the way into white.
  • Three camera modes are drafts: Soft, High Contrast and HDR Tone. Grainy has no fit yet.
  • The Linux and Windows versions are new and little tested. Import from the camera by USB is for the Mac only.
